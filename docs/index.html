Features

Loudness, without a plugin window.

Momentary, short-term and integrated loudness plus sample peak, measured locally and compared with the target you choose.

Momentary, short-term, integrated

Read 400 ms, 3 s and since-reset loudness in LUFS. K-weighting is computed for your actual sample rate, with absolute and relative gating for integrated values.

Podcast, streaming, broadcast targets

Choose -16, -14 or -23 LUFS. The readout turns green, amber or red relative to the target, and a marker shows it on the meter.

Peak with hold marker

A thin bar tracks sample peak in dBFS and keeps a hold tick so you can spot short spikes. Silence is shown as a dash.

Mic or system audio

Switch the source between your microphone and system audio. If Screen Recording is denied, microphone mode keeps working.

Light and resilient

Only block energies are stored, so memory stays small. Capture rebuilds on device or sample rate changes and restarts after sleep.

Menu bar text or icon

Show the LUFS value in the menu bar or an icon only, choose a 10, 15 or 30 fps refresh rate, and launch at login. Reset and Pause are one click away.

Install

Up and running in a minute.

LoudnessGlance is open source and not notarized with a paid Apple Developer ID, so macOS needs one extra step the first time.

  1. Download LoudnessGlance-macOS.zip and unzip it.
  2. Move LoudnessGlance.app to your Applications folder.
  3. Clear the quarantine flag once, then right-click the app and choose Open:
    xattr -cr /Applications/LoudnessGlance.app
  4. Grant Microphone access when prompted, or enable LoudnessGlance under System Settings Privacy Security Screen Recording for system audio, then reopen the app.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is LoudnessGlance really free and open source?

Yes. It is released under the MIT license, has no ads or subscriptions, and the full source is on GitHub. If it is useful to you, there is an optional Support button that opens Ko-fi.

What permissions does it need?

Microphone access when the source is Microphone, and Screen Recording when the source is System Audio, because macOS requires it for audio capture. Only audio is used, video frames are discarded, and Accessibility access is never requested.

Where is my data stored?

Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere. Audio is analyzed in memory on your Mac, and only your settings, such as source and target, are saved in local app preferences.

Why isn't the app notarized?

Notarization requires a paid Apple Developer account, which this free project does not have. Right-click the app and choose Open once to launch it, or build it yourself from source with Xcode.
